Why airport security wait times matter

Most travelers do not need a perfect forecast. They need a fast answer to a practical question: should I leave now, wait 20 minutes, or build extra buffer into my trip? This page is designed for that decision.

Airport-specific pages matter because one airport can be calm while another is spiking, and one terminal can move faster than another within the same city. That is why TSA Tracker keeps the airport hub, the airport pages, and the live charts connected instead of hiding everything behind one generic number.

How to use live wait times well

  • Check the page 30 to 60 minutes before you leave so the data is close to your departure time.
  • Use the history chart to spot a rising pattern instead of looking only at the current number.
  • Open the airport page when you need terminal, PreCheck, or official airport link context.
  • If the wait is high, add buffer. If it is low, still leave room for parking, terminal walking, and bag drop.

Frequently asked questions

How often does TSA Tracker update airport security wait times?

Approximately every 2 minutes for live airports, with timestamps shown so you can judge freshness before you leave.

Should I use the homepage or the airport page?

Use the homepage for a broad scan. Use the airport page when you already know where you are flying and want the most specific wait-time context.

What if my airport is not live yet?

Use the airport index and methodology page first. TSA Tracker only marks an airport live when the source is official and consistently reachable.

What should I check before leaving for the airport?

Check the current wait, the history chart, your terminal, and any PreCheck or CLEAR lane notes that apply to your trip.
